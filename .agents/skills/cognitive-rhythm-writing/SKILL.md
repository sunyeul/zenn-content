# Cognitive Rhythm Writing

## Overview

Vary the mental action the reader performs across a piece: observe, question, predict, revise, understand, and verify or act. Treat rhythm as movement in attention and judgment, not decorative sentence-length variation.

## Preserve the Content Contract

Before editing, identify the draft’s audience, purpose, evidence, required structure, tone, and length constraints. Preserve technical terms, citations, numbers, code, and supported claim strength. When another writing skill invokes this one, keep its content and verification requirements authoritative; apply this pass after the structure and claims are stable. Improving readability does not by itself justify adding material.

Use a light pass for reference material, API documentation, procedures, and lookup-oriented sections. Scannability and direct answers matter more there than narrative tension.

For Japanese prose, read [references/japanese-naturalness.md](references/japanese-naturalness.md) after the cognitive-state pass. Treat its patterns as contextual signals, not automatic errors.

## Workflow

1. Map short sections to the reader’s dominant action and the question they answer.
2. Find repeated explanation, assertion, or enumeration, plus transitions where the subject, data, or comparison changes without notice.
3. Repair the highest-impact runs with the smallest useful change: reorder, replace, or cut before adding a bridge. Keep necessary definitions and interpretation.
4. Re-read for factual fidelity and forward motion, then tune the voice across openings, results, caveats, and ending. Friendly sentence endings alone do not create rhythm.

| Reader action | Typical material | Useful transition |
|---|---|---|
| Observe | concrete experience, symptom, example | expose what does not yet fit |
| Question | unresolved cause, trade-off, or gap | delay only the information needed to answer it |
| Predict | plausible expectation or attempted approach | compare it with evidence or outcome |
| Revise | counterexample, surprise, changed judgment | state what the change means |
| Understand | explanation, concept, mechanism | connect it to the earlier problem |
| Verify / act | test, implication, reader check | return to the opening situation or next action |

Do not force every state or a fixed sequence. Use the map to diagnose monotony, not to impose a formula.

## Repair Patterns

### Keep one useful question in motion

Open a concrete question the draft genuinely answers and reveal the answer in stages. An example may move from expectation to execution to observation and interpretation, but do not force that sequence into every section. Close the question with a supported answer; an instruction to inspect a chart or a list of limitations is not a substitute. Never manufacture mystery or hide a prerequisite.

### Create the need before naming the concept

Show a concrete task, input, or missing distinction before introducing its label. Replace abstract explanation rather than stacking an example on top of it. Keep definition-first order when the reader is looking up a term or needs it to follow instructions.

### Alternate footing and revision

Use firm claims to establish footing. Add expectation, doubt, failed intuition, limitation, or counterexample only when supported by the writer's reasoning or evidence. Then state the revised conclusion clearly. Do not hedge settled facts or invent personal hesitation.

### Reconnect lists to concrete context

Keep lists when comparison, completeness, or scanning benefits from them. Let their items carry the connection to the concrete example where possible; add a sentence only if the meaning remains unclear. Avoid repeating the same agenda in the introduction, section openings, and conclusion, or adding identical result/limit commentary after every list.

### Remove self-narration, preserve judgment

Cut transitions that merely report progress when the headings already signal it. Preserve substantive boundaries: a new dataset, target, baseline, or evaluation scope must be recognizable. Do not make separate demonstrations read as one continuous validation, or treat differences across datasets as a controlled comparison. A useful bridge explains what changes and why it matters.

### Return to the concrete at the end

Return to the question promised by the title and opening, state what the evidence answered, and give a concrete next action. Keep new material and planned follow-up work outside that promise. Leave a residual question only when it is real and useful.

## Output Modes

- For diagnosis, provide a compact state map and rank the few flattest passages.
- For revision, return the revised prose and briefly note only consequential structural changes.
- For drafting, apply the rhythm silently unless the user asks for an audit.

## Final Audit

- Do adjacent sections require any meaningful change in attention or judgment?
- Does each open question close, narrow, or remain open for an explicit reason?
- Did any edit fabricate uncertainty, suspense, experience, or evidence?
- Are lists and headings still useful for scanning?
- Did the revision reduce reading effort, or merely add friendly explanations? Can repeated definitions, summaries, or transitions be cut without losing meaning?
- Are changes of example or evaluation scope explicit, without overstating continuity?
- Does the ending return to something concrete instead of dissolving into a generic moral?

Prefer a slightly uneven but truthful rhythm over a visibly engineered pattern.
